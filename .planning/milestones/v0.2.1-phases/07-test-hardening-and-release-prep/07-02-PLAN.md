<objective>
Make `sh tests/run.sh` pass on a machine that has `bbjcpl` or `bbjcplw` on `PATH` (MAINT-06,
review WR-01, decisions D-11 to D-13). A shared helper in `tests/lib.sh` scrubs `PATH` and the
other discovery sources for the no-route gates only. A new CI-run test proves the scrub is
load-bearing with a fake compiler, and needs no real BBj.

Purpose: the owner's Windows and macOS machines (Phase 9) have `bbjcpl` on `PATH`. Without the
scrub, 41 gates fail there: 35 in `test_noroute_notice.sh` and 6 in `test_exit_contract.sh`.

Decision on research open question 2 (recorded here, Claude's discretion within D-11): the helper
replaces a compiler-holding directory with a per-call symlink farm under `$WORK` that links
everything in it except the four compiler names. It does not drop the whole directory. D-11's
stated intent is "other tools (`sh`, `awk`, `curl`, `python3`, `busybox`) stay where they are".
Dropping a shared directory such as `/usr/local/bin` or a Homebrew prefix would remove those tools.
The farm idiom already exists in the repo (`$WORK/farm` in `tests/test_install_codex.sh` and
`tests/test_ci_gates.sh`). Plain removal remains only as the fallback when `WORK` is unset.

Output: `no_compiler_path` and `no_compiler_env` in `tests/lib.sh`, the call sites in the two
no-route files and on the `SYSPATH` of `tests/test_tier2_fake.sh`, the new
`tests/test_path_scrub.sh` (14 gates, prefix `pathscrub_`), and the No-route sweep below. The
sweep classes every test file that names the compiler or runs the hook (D-12).
</objective>
